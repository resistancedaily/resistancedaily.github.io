  • Focus · Sudan

    Sudan's long war

    Since 15 April 2023, fighting between Sudan's army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces has killed at least 59,000 people by one count, and possibly far more, and driven about 13 million from their homes. Both sides deny targeting civilians. The war now reaches into Abyei and South Sudan.

  • Focus · Upper Siang, Arunachal Pradesh

    The fight over the Siang dam

    Villages along the Siang oppose an 11,000 MW dam that would submerge 27 villages. After arson in Pasighat on 23 October, police say 69 people have been arrested; on 31 October Delhi Police detained protesters outside Arunachal Bhawan, who allege sexual assault.

  • Focus · Tibet

    Tibet: schools, faith and who chooses the next Dalai Lama

    UN experts say about a million Tibetan children are in state boarding schools; China says they serve children in remote areas. Exile officials report school closures, a five-year sentence for prayers and demolitions at a monastery in recent weeks. Beijing insists it must approve the next Dalai Lama.

  • Focus · Democratic Republic of the Congo

    Congo's mines and the minerals in our devices

    Congo mines about three-quarters of the world's cobalt and much of its coltan, much of it dug by hand. Children have been documented in the pits, an armed group taxes mines in the east, and courts are testing who is responsible further down the supply chain.

  • Focus · Indonesia

    Smoke since August

    Fires in Kalimantan and Sumatra have burned more than 1.9 million hectares this year, a satellite monitor says, and sent smoke across Southeast Asia. Indonesia's government calls the haze largely natural; researchers and environmental groups say people lit the fires.

  • Focus · Brazil

    Brazil's vote: what's at stake for rights

    Lula da Silva won Brazil's 25 October runoff by 51.8% to 48.2%. His rival first said he would seek to annul the vote; his party then accepted the result. The next term inherits fights over the Amazon, Indigenous land and deadly policing.

  • Focus · India and the world

    Who decides what you see online

    This month India's IT ministry asked Meta to change what Facebook and Instagram recommend about protests, and posts covering them were blocked. Governments and the companies themselves have done this before.
